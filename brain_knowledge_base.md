## 3. TRAINING RULES (LOCKED)

### Rule 1: Cadence Self-Selection (Pace-Gated)
- Zone 1-2 (easy/long runs): Allow cadence to self-select. Natural cadence at 6:20-6:30/km is ~164 spm — this is the metabolic optimum at that speed. Do NOT force 170+ spm. Forcing higher cadence at Z2 pace increases HR without increasing pace = higher metabolic cost, no benefit.
- Threshold pace (5:00-5:30/km): Nudge toward 170-175 spm if feeling heavy.
- Race/interval pace (< 4:45/km): Target 175-182 spm. Higher cadence reduces overstriding braking force.
- Evidence: Jun 14, 2026 — HR rose without pace change when cadence was forced upward at 6:22/km.

### Rule 2: Warmup Patience
- First kilometer of every run must stay under 130 bpm.
- Allow cardiac ramp to complete before entering Zone 2 effort.
- Evidence: Jun 14 Lap 1 at 7:09/km (122 bpm) produced 13 consecutive km of clean zone control.

### Rule 3: Thermal HR Correction
- All outdoor HR data in Manila (April-November) requires thermal correction before analysis.
- Standard correction: subtract 5-8 bpm from observed HR to get neutral-equivalent effort.
- Do not penalize pace or flag zone breaches without applying correction first.

### Rule 4: Speed Reserve Paradigm (Primary Training Philosophy)
- Marathon pace (4:15/km for sub-3) = % of absolute speed ceiling.
- Sub-3 requires 5k pace to drop to 3:55-4:10/km to make 4:15/km feel aerobically manageable (~80% of ceiling).
- Speed sessions (Fartlek, strides, VO2 Max intervals) are NON-NEGOTIABLE deep into marathon block.
- Evidence: Kipchoge, Kiptum, Kejelcha, Sawe, Kiplimo, PH record holder — all 5k/10k specialists before marathon debut.

### Rule 5: Accumulated Fatigue Protocol (Pre-Fatigue Long Run)
- Long runs are always preceded by a moderate Legs + Core gym session the evening prior.
- Objective: Force cardiovascular system to operate under muscular fatigue, mimicking km 30-42 of marathon.
- Evidence: Jun 13 Legs (LP 123 kg) then Jun 14 Long Run (15 km, pace held in final 3 laps).
- Do NOT apply this protocol in recovery weeks or when Achilles is symptomatic.
- **INTENSITY CAP (Updated Jul 19, 2026):** Pre-Fatigue Leg session must be 60-70% of max load. NO failure sets. DOMS-inducing volume is a protocol violation. Target is neuromuscular fatigue (heavy legs), NOT structural soreness (pain at km 1). If athlete is debating stopping within first 12 km due to leg pain, the pre-fatigue was too aggressive.
- **Walking Load Modifier:** If athlete has significant incidental walking volume on the pre-fatigue day (e.g., going out all day), reduce gym load by additional 20-30%. Walking + heavy gym + long run = excessive accumulated fatigue.
- **Non-negotiable exception to load cap:** Seated Calf Raises (4x20 @ 70kg, 3s eccentric) remain at full load regardless — this is tendon bulletproofing, not muscle fatigue induction.

### Rule 6: Achilles Bulletproofing Protocol
- Seated Calf Raises: 4x20 at 70 kg, 3-second eccentric (lowering phase) is standard practice in Legs + Core sessions.
- Background: This is a preventative bulletproofing protocol, NOT rehab for a weak Achilles. The user has excellent structural integrity. An isolated flare-up in early June 2026 was solely the result of poor programming (a heavy leg session immediately following two half-marathons in consecutive weeks).
- Objective: Maintain elite tendon stiffness and elasticity for long-distance durability.

### Rule 7: Fueling — Fuel the Work, Restrict the Rest
- Runs < 90 min: Semi-fasted running approved. Eat post-run. Drives fat oxidation adaptation.
- Runs >= 90 min: Mandatory 30-40g carbs 45-60 min pre-run. Fuel at 45-50 min mid-run.
- Caloric deficit must come from meals furthest from training windows, never from reducing around sessions.
- Protein: Keep at a realistic floor of **105–115g daily** (1.5–1.7g/kg for 68.9 kg) with current home foods (eggs, Greek yogurt, canned tuna), scaling to 125–140g once protein powder is introduced. Heavy lifting + restricted calories = muscle catabolism risk without adequate protein.
- **Race Weight Target ($\le$ 68.0 kg) Protocol:** Until the athlete reaches $\le$ 68.0 kg (0.9 kg remaining as of Aug 19, 2026), every daily briefing must set a specific caloric intake ceiling and deficit goal. On rest and low-volume recovery days, enforce a strict **1,800–2,000 kcal intake ceiling** to prevent rest-day overeating from erasing training-day deficits.

### Rule 8: The Muscular vs. Cardiovascular Limiter (Tired Legs Protocol)
- **The Rule:** Never force the pace just to hit a Zone 2 heart rate target.
- If your legs are heavily fatigued (e.g., post-gym or late in a block) and you cannot reach 162 bpm without running at a pace that feels mechanically unsafe or exhausting, **let the HR stay in Zone 1**. 
- In this state, your muscular system is the limiter, not your cardiovascular system. Pushing the pace forces bad biomechanics and invites injury. A Zone 1 recovery run is still highly beneficial.

### Rule 9: Zone 2 is the DEFAULT — Zone 1 is the Exception (Non-Negotiable)
- **Zone 2 (162–174 bpm) is the default prescription for ALL non-recovery aerobic runs.** It is the primary aerobic development band for marathon training: mitochondrial density, fat oxidation, cardiac stroke volume.
- **162 bpm is the FLOOR of Zone 2, NOT a ceiling to stay under.** This was a documented coaching error (Jun 22–25, 2026) where Zone 1 was over-prescribed out of habit.
- **Zone 1 (< 162 bpm) is ONLY appropriate under the following specific conditions:**
  1. ACWR > 1.40 (structural deload is active)
  2. Outdoor run with heat index > 35°C (thermal cardiac drift makes Zone 2 effort unmeasurable)
  3. Explicitly designated recovery flush day (the day immediately after a TE 4.0+ session)
  4. Rule 8 applies: muscular system is the mechanical limiter, not the cardiovascular system
- **Any agent prescribing Zone 1 outside of these four conditions is in violation of this rule.**
