# Speed & Endurance Development
*Evidence-Based Workouts for Running Faster and Longer*

## 1. Core Principle: Train the Right Energy Systems

Running performance at every distance is governed by three energy systems:
| System | Duration | Primary Fuel | Key Workout |
|---|---|---|---|
| **ATP-PCr (Alactic)** | 0–10 sec | Phosphocreatine | Sprints, strides |
| **Glycolytic (Lactic)** | 10 sec–2 min | Glycogen (fast) | 400m–800m reps |
| **Oxidative (Aerobic)** | 2 min+ | Fat + glycogen | Tempo, long runs |

For a runner chasing both speed AND endurance, all three must be trained — but in the right proportions. Follow the **80/20 rule** (80% aerobic volume, 20% intensity) as the distribution framework.

---

## 2. The Non-Negotiable Foundation: Aerobic Volume

**You cannot shortcut the aerobic base.** Speed development is only sustainable if built on a large aerobic foundation. An underdeveloped aerobic base means:
- Faster lactic acid accumulation at race pace
- Slower recovery between hard sessions
- Higher injury risk from compensatory mechanics

**Minimum weekly volume for meaningful speed development:** 40–60 km/week of mostly Zone 2 running.

---

## 3. The Speed Development Hierarchy

Work these sessions in order of priority within your weekly block:

### A. Strides (Neuromuscular Primer)
- **What:** 6–10 × 80–100m accelerations to ~95% effort, full recovery (90 sec walk between)
- **When:** 2–3× per week, tacked onto the END of easy runs
- **Purpose:** Teaches fast-twitch recruitment without accumulating lactic acid. Improves stride efficiency, turnover, and coordination.
- **Key rule:** Stay RELAXED. This is not a sprint. Drive elbows, lift knees, let the acceleration happen naturally.
- **Cadence target:** ≥ 180 spm by the end of each stride

### B. Hill Sprints (Alactic Power & Economy)
- **What:** 8–10 × 10-second ALL-OUT uphill sprints. Walk back down and wait a full 2 minutes between reps.
- **When:** Once a week (can replace flat strides). Best done at the end of a long easy run or on a dedicated speed day.
- **Purpose:** Develops explosive power, increases running efficiency/economy, and forces perfect knee-drive mechanics.
- **Advantage over Flat Sprints:** Much lower impact forces. You cannot run as fast uphill, significantly reducing injury risk to hamstrings and Achilles, while still maximizing heart rate and fast-twitch muscle recruitment.
- **Key rule:** Do not cut the 2-minute rest short. This is an alactic (ATP-PCr) system workout; you need full phosphocreatine replenishment between sprints.

### C. VO2 Max Intervals (Critical Speed Development)
- **What:** 5–8 × 600m–1200m at ~5K pace with 1:1 work-to-rest ratio
- **When:** Once per week, only after completing a proper warm-up
- **Purpose:** Raises the ceiling — elevates maximal oxygen uptake so all paces feel easier.
- **Heart rate target:** Zone 4 (for this athlete: 182–191 bpm) by the end of each rep
- **Pace target:** 4:30–5:00 /km depending on heat and fatigue
- **Key rule:** The last rep should feel like the hardest, but form should NOT break down. If you are collapsing, the rep length is too long.
- **Progressive overload:** Add one rep every 2 weeks, not more pace.

### D. Threshold / Tempo Runs (Lactate Clearance)
- **What:** 20–40 minutes of sustained running at "comfortably hard" effort
- **When:** Once per week, typically mid-week
- **Purpose:** Raises the lactate threshold — the pace you can sustain before lactic acid accumulates faster than it clears.
- **Heart rate target:** Zone 3 upper (175–181 bpm for this athlete)
- **Pace target:** ~5:20–5:50 /km (heat-adjusted)
- **Variants:**
  - *Classic tempo:* Single sustained block (25–40 min)
  - *Cruise intervals:* 3–4 × 10–12 min with 2 min easy jog between (more manageable in heat)
  - *Progressive tempo:* Start at the bottom of Zone 3 and drift to the top over 30 min

### D. Long Run (Aerobic Ceiling & Fat Adaptation)
- **What:** 75–120 min at Zone 2 (162–174 bpm for this athlete)
- **When:** Once per week, typically on the weekend
- **Purpose:** Builds mitochondrial density, fat oxidation capacity, glycogen sparing, and mental durability.
- **Key protocol:** Run the first 70% strictly in Zone 2. The final 30% can drift to Zone 3 (marathon effort) to simulate race-end fatigue.
- **HR ceiling:** NEVER exceed 162 bpm for the first 70% of the long run. Speed is irrelevant — the adaptation is in the aerobic HR range.
- **Distance target:** 14–22 km depending on weekly volume and recovery status

### E. Hill Repeats (Power & Leg Strength)
- **What:** 8–12 × 30–60 sec hard uphill effort, walk or jog back down
- **When:** Every 10–14 days as a substitute for flat VO2 intervals
- **Purpose:** Builds leg-specific strength, improves VO2 max, and carries zero eccentric (downhill) stress — safer than flat speed work.
- **Key rule:** Lean into the hill from the ankles, drive knees high, pump arms aggressively.

## 4. Weekly Structure for Speed + Endurance (Sample 5-Day Block)

| Day | Session | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Monday | Full Rest or Mobility | Recovery |
| Tuesday | Easy run (45–55 min) + 6 strides | Neuromuscular primer |
| Wednesday | VO2 Max Intervals | Speed ceiling |
| Thursday | Easy run (40 min) | Recovery volume |
| Friday | Tempo run / Cruise intervals | Lactate threshold |
| Saturday | Full Rest |  |
| Sunday | Long Run (90–110 min, Zone 2) | Aerobic base |

---

## 5. Progression Rules

1. **Never increase both volume AND intensity in the same week.** Pick one.
2. **Every 4th week: reduce volume by 30%.** This is a recovery week — do NOT skip it.
3. **Strides first, intervals second.** If you haven't done consistent strides for 2+ weeks, don't jump to full VO2 intervals.
4. **Performance beats schedule.** If HRV drops below floor or RHR spikes, default to easy running regardless of what the plan says.

---

## 6. Key Metrics to Track Progress

| Metric | Target Trend |
|---|---|
| Pace at Zone 2 HR | Gets faster over 8–12 weeks |
| Cadence at race pace | ≥ 178 spm |
| HRV 7-day avg | Stable or rising |
| Vertical oscillation | Decreasing (< 8 cm target) |
| Ground contact time | Decreasing (< 250 ms target) |
