# Aerobic Maintenance & Weight Loss for Runners
*Scientifically Supported Protocols for Base Fitness, Body Recomposition, and High-Readiness States*

---

## 1. The "90% Ready" Concept (High-Readiness State)

A high-readiness state means maintaining a physical plateau where you are **4–6 weeks away from peak race fitness** at any time. Trying to stay at 100% peak fitness year-round is biologically impossible and leads to injury, hormonal suppression (overtraining syndrome), and mental burnout.

### The Science of Training Detraining & Maintenance
- **Aerobic Capacity (VO2 Max):** Can be maintained for up to 15 weeks with a **volume reduction of up to 60%**, *provided that training intensity (frequency of fast runs) is preserved* (Mujika & Padilla, 2001).
- **Structural Durability (Bone/Tendon Stiffness):** Requires consistent weekly loading. Tendon stiffness and bone mineral density start to decay after **10–14 days** of complete rest or severe volume drop.
- **Glycogen Storage Capacity:** Drops quickly within 7–10 days of inactivity but is preserved easily with a moderate running volume.

## 2. The Sweet Spot: Maintenance Weekly Mileage

For your physiological profile (historical peak of 50–68 km/week), your optimal **"Pounding Pavement" Maintenance Volume** is:

### **35 – 45 km per week**

#### Why this range is optimal:
1. **Preserves 95% of Aerobic Fitness:** It keeps your stroke volume (heart chamber size) and capillary density at their peak.
2. **Maintains Tendon Stiffness:** Regular impact at 40 km/week keeps your calves, Achilles, and plantar fascia conditioned to absorb landing forces.
3. **Avoids Systemic Fatigue:** Your stress hormones (cortisol) stay low, sleep quality stays high, and recovery is complete.
4. **Quick Transition:** You can ramp from 40 km/week to a 60 km/week peak race build in just **4 weeks** without injuring yourself.

## 4. The Science of Weight Loss & Body Recomposition for Runners

For a runner targeting **68–72 kg** to maximize running efficiency (reducing body mass directly increases VO2 max relative to weight), the weight loss must be managed carefully to avoid losing muscle mass or crashing training performance.

### A. The Energy Cost of Bodyweight
- Every **1 kg** of excess bodyweight lost translates to an automatic saving of **1 to 2 seconds per kilometer** at marathon effort (due to reduced oxygen requirements for carrying mass).
- Dropping from 76 kg to 70 kg (~8% weight drop) can improve your running economy and pace by **6–12 seconds/km** at the exact same heart rate.

### B. The Cortisol Trap (Why Running Too Much Stalls Weight Loss)
Many runners increase volume to 60+ km/week to burn calories, but find their weight *stalls* or increases.
* **Mechanism:** Extreme training volume combined with a steep calorie deficit spikes **cortisol** (the stress hormone).
* High cortisol causes **water retention** (masking fat loss), **suppresses thyroid function** (lowering metabolic rate), and triggers **intense sugar cravings** that lead to bingeing.
* **The Solution:** Keep running volume moderate (35–45 km/week) and supplement with low-impact Zone 2 cycling. This keeps stress hormones low, allowing the body to easily release fat.

### C. Fueling and Macronutrient Rules
1. **The Caloric Deficit Ceiling:** Do not exceed a **300–500 kcal daily deficit**. A steeper deficit will trigger muscle wasting and tank your HRV.
2. **Protein Targeting:** Consume **1.6 to 2.0 grams of protein per kilogram of bodyweight** per day (approx. 115g–145g of protein daily for you). High protein prevents muscle loss during caloric deficits and preserves fast-twitch fibers.
3. **Periodized Fueling ("Fuel for the Work Required"):**
   - *On easy run & cycle days:* Keep carbs moderate; rely on fat oxidation.
   - *On interval & tempo days:* Consume carbs 2 hours before the session. You must have glycogen to hit high intensities safely.

## 5. Ramping from Maintenance to Race-Ready (4-Week Pivot)

If you find a race and decide to compete, pivot your training like this:

* **Week 1:** Increase weekly volume to **48 km**. Extend the long run to **18 km**. Keep speedwork to 1 session.
* **Week 2:** Increase weekly volume to **54 km**. Extend the long run to **22 km**. Add a marathon-pace block inside the long run.
* **Week 3 (Peak Week):** Increase weekly volume to **60 km**. Extend the long run to **26 km**.
* **Week 4 (Taper & Race):** Drop running volume by 50% (30 km total). Keep speedwork light. Race on fresh legs.

## 6. Extended LSD Protocol (>21 km) in Maintenance

To preserve elite-level structural endurance and psychological comfort with half-marathon and marathon-plus distances while maintaining a moderate weekly volume (35–45 km), you can safely integrate extended **Long Slow Distance (LSD) runs of 22–26 km** using a periodized rotation.

### A. The Rotational Strategy (Bi-Weekly Cycle)
When weekly volume is 40 km, a single 24 km long run represents **60% of your weekly volume**. Running this weekly will cause orthopedic breakdown. Instead, alternate your weekends:

* **Week A (Aerobic Maintenance):** Easy Long Run of **12–15 km** (Zone 2). Preserves aerobic enzymes with low recovery cost.
* **Week B (Extended Durability):** Extended LSD of **22–26 km** (Zone 2 strictly). Re-stimulates deep structural durability and fat oxidation.

This bi-weekly alternation allows your joints, ligaments, and glycogen reserves to completely recover between high-stress orthopedic loads.

### B. Mandatory Prerequisites (The "Green Light" Checklist)
Never attempt an extended LSD (>21 km) in maintenance unless you check all four boxes:

1. **Autonomic Balance (HRV):** Your 7-day RMSSD rolling average must be fully within your healthy baseline (**>116 ms**), and last night's HRV must not be severely suppressed.
2. **Resting Heart Rate (RHR):** Stable and resting in your normal **38–42 bpm** range. A spike of +3–5 bpm above normal indicates acute systemic fatigue.
3. **Orthopedic Integrity:** Zero joint soreness, tendon stiffness (especially Achilles or plantar fascia), or muscle strains.
4. **Thermal Index:** High heat and humidity (typical in Marikina/UPD) drastically increase cardiac cost. Avoid extended runs >21 km if the heat index exceeds **38°C** at the time of your run. Swap to an early morning window or indoors if possible.

### C. Fueling and Intra-Workout Protocol
Extended LSD sessions are massive depletion events. To protect muscle mass (crucial for weight loss/recomposition) and avoid the "bonk":

* **Pre-Workout:** Consume **75–100g of complex carbohydrates** 2 to 3 hours before starting. 
* **Intra-Workout Carb Target:** Consume **60 to 80 grams of fast-acting carbohydrates per hour** (via gels, sports drinks, or chews) starting at minute 30. Do not attempt to run >21 km "fasted" or low-carb; this triggers massive cortisol spikes and muscle wasting.
* **Hydration:** Consume **500–750ml of water with electrolytes** per hour in tropical humidity.
* **Post-Workout Recovery Bolus:** Consume **30g of high-quality protein** alongside **80–100g of carbohydrates** within 45 minutes of finishing to instantly halt muscle breakdown and accelerate glycogen replacement.

### D. Critical Scheduling Adjustments
An extended LSD shifts your training stress. Make these immediate adjustments to the surrounding days:

1. **The 48h Recovery Window:** The 24 hours before and 24 hours after a >21 km LSD must be strictly low-stress.
   - *Day Before:* Rest or 30-40 min very easy recovery spin/jog.
   - *Day After:* STRICT REST or 45 min very light recovery cycling (Zone 1, HR < 135 bpm).
2. **Zone 2 Ceiling is Absolute:** Run the entire extended distance strictly inside **Zone 2 (145–162 bpm)**. No tempo or marathon-pace finishes on Week B extended days. Adding speed to distance in a maintenance phase is an unnecessary stress compound.
3. **Cadence Awareness:** As fatigue sets in after km 18, cadence naturally drops. Keep focus on maintaining a light, quick turnover (**≥ 168 spm**) to avoid heavy heel-striking as you fatigue.
