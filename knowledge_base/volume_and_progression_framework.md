# Volume & Progression Framework
*Evidence-Based Protocol for Mileage and Time-on-Feet Progression*

## 1. The Concept of "Baseline Mileage"
Your **Baseline Mileage** (or Baseline Minutes) is the highest weekly volume you can sustain week after week without accumulating meaningful physical fatigue, soreness, or the mental burden of "grinding" through training.

- **It is a Range, Not a Fixed Number:** For example, a baseline of 35–45 miles (or ~4–5 hours) per week means you can fluctuate within this band safely.
- **Below Baseline:** You are underloaded and losing aerobic adaptations.
- **Above Baseline:** The stress-to-recovery equation tips, requiring careful management (consolidation).

### How to Find Your Baseline
Review your last 6–12 months of training and ask:
1. At what volume did I feel strong and consistent without dreading runs or waking up with heavy legs?
2. At what point did the mileage start costing me? (e.g., minor niggles, dropped motivation, extreme fatigue).
3. The upper limit of Question 1 and the threshold of Question 2 is your baseline anchor. Everything else builds from there.

## 2. Why the "10% Rule" Fails
The traditional rule of "don't increase weekly mileage by more than 10%" is fundamentally flawed because it applies a single mathematical percentage across all fitness states, ignoring the physiological reality of the runner.

### Failure 1: When Rebuilding (Below Baseline)
If you are coming back from rest or injury and are at 15 miles/week, a 10% increase is 1.5 miles. Split across 4 runs, that is less than a half-mile per run. It is a "rounding error" and provides zero adaptive stimulus.
- **The Fix:** When rebuilding *below* your baseline, you can safely increase volume aggressively by **20–30% per week** (e.g., 15mi → 25mi → 30mi). Your body has handled this volume before; it is simply returning to homeostasis.

### Failure 2: When Peaking (Above Baseline)
If your baseline is 40 miles/week and you apply the 10% rule (44mi → 48mi → 53mi), you are aggressively paddling new stress onto a system operating above its comfortable load. A mile at 120% of your baseline is a vastly different physiological event than a mile at 70%.
- **The Fix:** When pushing *above* your baseline ceiling, increases should be much smaller (**5–10%**) and held for longer durations before increasing again.

---

## 3. The Consolidation Protocol (Flat Weeks)
When pushing your volume to new heights (above baseline) during a marathon block, you cannot climb every single week.

**The Strategy:** Apply 5–10% increases every 2 to 3 weeks, surrounded by "Flat Weeks" (e.g., 42, 42, 45, 45, 48).

**Why Flat Weeks Are Critical:**
- Flat weeks are not wasted weeks. They are **Consolidation Weeks**.
- Cardiovascular fitness improves much faster than structural fitness. 
- During a flat week, your connective tissues (tendons, ligaments) catch up to the cardiovascular fitness you just gained. Bone density responds to the new load.
- *Rule of thumb:* If accumulation outpaces consolidation, the injury mechanism engages.

---

## 4. Race Selection and Reality
If you cannot safely reach the required time and training block volume for a target race given your current baseline (and the slow rate at which you can build above it), **you are not ready for that race yet.**
Forcing a compressed timeline to hit an arbitrary race date is the fastest route to injury and burnout. Pick a race timeline that matches what your body is able to absorb.
